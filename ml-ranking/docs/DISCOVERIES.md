# Discoveries

Cohort: 87,921 CVEs published 2024-01 → 2025-12, snapshotted at their publication month end, with
122 later KEV additions (base rate 0.139%). Every rate has a Wilson 95% interval; cells with fewer
than 20 observations are excluded from headline claims. Generated by `python -m pipeline.discoveries`;
machine-readable output in `artifacts/discoveries.json`.

---

## 1. The blind spot is real and large

**On the Jan–Sep 2025 pool (52 exploited CVEs), the three published baselines' top-500 lists cover
only 19 of them. 33 exploited CVEs (63%) appear in none of the three lists.** Their median CVSS is
7.8 and 100% have EPSS < 0.1.

This is the central finding. The competition's answer key is dominated by vulnerabilities that
severity-first and EPSS-first ranking *cannot* see, because on the day they were published they had
no exploit code and no meaningful EPSS. Any solution that just re-sorts on those two fields is
competing for the same 19 hits.

Our ranking finds 24 of the 52, including 8 of those 33 blind-spot CVEs — the ones no conventional
ranking surfaces at all.

Caveat: 52 positives is a small pool; the 63% figure carries wide uncertainty, but the qualitative
pattern is stable across the 2024 and 2025 pools.

## 2. Public exploit code is the strongest observable — and it is late

| Public exploit sources at snapshot | CVEs | later KEV rate |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 85,741 | 0.09% |
| 1 | 1,957 | 1.58% |
| 2 | 213 | 8.92% |

An 18× jump from zero to one source, and ~100× from zero to two. But only 2.5% of candidates have any
public exploit code at snapshot, so this signal ranks a tiny corner of the pool. The model's job is
the other 97.5%.

## 3. EPSS is calibrated but nearly empty in this cohort

Observed KEV rate rises monotonically with EPSS: 0.10% at EPSS 0, 0.39% under 1%, 1.9% at 1–10%,
4.0% at 10–50%, and 15.4% above 50%. Where the score is non-zero it is well calibrated. The problem
is coverage: **99.9% of candidates sit below 0.10 at snapshot** (median EPSS 0.0004), so an EPSS-first
ordering can only rank a tiny corner of the pool — which is exactly why it finds 8 of 52 exploited
CVEs. A model that only re-sorts on EPSS inherits that blind spot.

## 4. Severity separates weakly, and expensively

CRITICAL CVEs are 0.538% likely to be exploited versus 0.052% for LOW — a 10.4× ratio, yet **99.5% of
CRITICAL CVEs are never exploited**. The ratio is real; the *precision* is useless. A CVSS-first
process spends all 500 slots at 1/200 accuracy, and on the 2025 pool finds 5 of 52 exploited CVEs.

## 5. Weakness class carries independent information

Highest-lift CWE classes (n ≥ 150): CWE-611 (XXE) **12.4×**, CWE-552 9.3×, CWE-290 8.9×,
CWE-306 (missing authentication) 8.6×, CWE-288 7.7×, CWE-367 7.1×. CWE-611 is exploited at 1.7%
against a 0.139% base rate.

Interpretation: weaknesses that cross a trust boundary and require no credentials are exploited out
of proportion to their severity scores.

## 6. Attackers return to vendors they already exploit

CVEs from a vendor with at least one prior KEV entry are exploited at 0.278% versus 0.102% — 2.7×.
This uses only KEV additions before each snapshot, so it is deployable knowledge, not hindsight.
It is also the reason vendor history is a feature rather than a curiosity.

## 7. Description wording predicts exploitation

| Phrase in description | lift | n |
|---|---|---|
| "XXE" / XML external entity | 13.0× | 228 |
| "authentication bypass" | 8.2× | 949 |
| "unauthenticated" | 5.4× | 6,542 |
| "third-party dependency" | 3.9× | 187 |
| XSS / CSRF phrasing | < 1× | 6k+ |

Descriptions are written at publication by the CNA, so this is available on day one — unlike EPSS or
exploit code.

## 8. IBM Granite similarity is a genuine, independent signal

Cosine similarity between a CVE description and the descriptions of KEV entries **already on the list
at snapshot time** reaches ROC-AUC 0.742 for predicting a later addition, using no CVSS, no
EPSS and no structured field. Exploitation clusters semantically: memory-corruption and
deserialisation descriptions sit near previously exploited CVEs.

## 9. The window that matters is days

For CVEs published 2023–2025 that reached KEV, 64% (2023), 61% (2024) and 74% (2025) were added
within 30 days of publication; median lag 7 days.

**Why this matters for the benchmark:** those fast additions are *already excluded* from the 2026
pool (the CVEs were on KEV by their snapshot month end). The competition is therefore not "who gets
exploited fast" — it is "who gets exploited *late*", which is structurally harder and is exactly the
population our label is built from.

## What we tested and rejected

- **CVSS × CWE interactions** added nothing measurable over the separate features on the held-out
  pool; they are retained in the model but do not carry the argument.
- **EPSS change over the previous month** is informative historically but is unavailable for the 2026
  pool (one snapshot per CVE), so it was demoted to an analysis, not a feature. Shipping a feature
  that is always NaN at prediction time would be a quiet form of leakage-by-unavailability.
- **CPE product features** show attractive lift but 49.6% of 2026 rows lack CPEs for reasons that
  correlate with NVD attention; excluded (see DATA_AUDIT.md).
- **Ransomware association** of a vendor's prior KEV entries is included but weak once vendor KEV
  count is controlled.
